# Helplines and Support Services for Women Facing Domestic Violence in India

> Corpus document for the Sahara knowledge base. Facts below are drawn from
> public Government of India schemes. Before any real-world deployment, have
> a partner organisation (e.g. a State Women's Commission or DLSA lawyer)
> verify and localise this document.

## Emergency numbers

- **112 — Emergency Response Support System (ERSS).** Single national
  emergency number for police, fire, and ambulance. Available 24x7 across
  India. Use in any situation of immediate physical danger.
- **181 — Women Helpline (WHL).** National helpline for women affected by
  violence. 24x7, toll-free, multilingual. Provides information, counselling
  referral, and connects callers to police, hospitals, legal aid, and the
  nearest One Stop Centre. A woman can call for herself or someone can call
  on her behalf.
- **1091 — Women Police Helpline.** Connects to women's police assistance
  in many cities and states.

## One Stop Centres (Sakhi / OSC)

One Stop Centres are run under a Ministry of Women and Child Development
scheme and exist in districts across India. A single facility provides,
under one roof:

- Medical assistance (linkage to hospital care)
- Police assistance (help registering complaints, including a Domestic
  Incident Report or FIR if the woman chooses)
- Psycho-social counselling
- Legal aid and counselling
- Temporary shelter (short stay)
- Video-conferencing facility for recording statements

A woman can walk in directly, be referred by the 181 helpline, or be brought
by police. Services are free. The 181 helpline can tell a caller the address
of the nearest One Stop Centre.

## Protection Officers (under PWDVA 2005)

Every district has Protection Officers appointed under the Protection of
Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005. A Protection Officer:

- Helps an aggrieved woman file a Domestic Incident Report (DIR)
- Helps her apply to the Magistrate for protection, residence, monetary,
  custody, or compensation orders
- Helps her access medical aid, shelter homes, and legal aid
- Can be contacted through the District Women and Child Development office,
  the 181 helpline, or a One Stop Centre

Filing a Domestic Incident Report does NOT require filing an FIR with the
police first. The woman chooses what action, if any, follows.

## Free legal aid (NALSA / DLSA)

Under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, every woman is entitled to
free legal aid in India regardless of income. The District Legal Services
Authority (DLSA) sits at every district court and provides:

- A free lawyer for proceedings under the PWDVA, maintenance cases,
  divorce, and criminal complaints
- Help drafting applications
- Lok Adalat and mediation services where appropriate

A woman can approach the DLSA front office at the district court directly,
or be referred through a One Stop Centre or Protection Officer.

## Medical care and medico-legal records

- Any government hospital must provide treatment to a woman who has been
  injured. She can ask the doctor to make a **medico-legal case (MLC)
  record** of her injuries.
- An MLC creates dated, official documentation of injuries and does not by
  itself require her to file an FIR or pursue any case. It preserves
  evidence for whenever, and if ever, she chooses to act.
- Under the PWDVA, the Magistrate, Protection Officer, or service provider
  can also direct medical examination and the medical facility must provide
  it.

## Shelter

Under the PWDVA, a woman has the right to be accommodated in a shelter home
on request. Shelter homes (including Swadhar Greh facilities) can be reached
through Protection Officers, One Stop Centres, or the 181 helpline. A
shelter cannot demand she file a police complaint as a condition of giving
shelter.

## In Telangana specifically

- **SHE Teams** — Telangana Police units focused on women's safety; they
  respond to harassment and safety complaints and can be reached through
  the Telangana Police.
- **Bharosa Centres** — integrated support centres for women and children
  in Hyderabad providing police, medical, legal, and counselling support in
  one location, similar in concept to One Stop Centres.
- The Telangana State Commission for Women receives complaints and can
  direct women to district-level services.
